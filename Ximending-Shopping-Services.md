## Shopping shortlist

**Top shop: Carrefour Guilin**, No. 1 Guilin Road, Wanhua—large one-stop selection of Taiwan cookies, nougat, pineapple cakes, instant noodles, dried fruit, tea and inexpensive gifts; normally 24 hours. It is best visited by a five-minute taxi rather than making Mom walk approximately 15–20 minutes each way. Major cards normally accepted.

Closer compact choices:

- **Ten Ren Tea, Ximending area:** boxed oolong/high-mountain tea and tea bags, roughly **NT$250–1,200**; chain shop, cards ordinarily accepted.
- **Kuo Yuan Ye:** established Taiwanese bakery for pineapple cakes and traditional pastries; typical gift boxes approximately **NT$400–800**. Confirm the nearest operating branch before departure.
- **Red House creative shops:** Taiwanese-designed stationery and small gifts; Sunday opening is generally around **11:00**, but individual studios vary.
- **9×9/POYA-type stationery store:** pens, stickers, notebooks and Taiwan-themed inexpensive gifts, usually **NT$30–300**.
- Sun cakes are a Taichung specialty; buy a sealed branded box rather than an anonymous tourist-market product.

Chain-store card acceptance is reliable; small Red House vendors may require cash. Taiwan’s official visitor refund requires **NT$2,000 from the same designated TRS store on the same day**, passport presentation and departure within 90 days. A shop merely accepting cards does not imply TRS participation. Official guidance: [Taiwan VAT refund](https://eng.taiwan.net.tw/m1.aspx?sNo=0024567).

## Drugstore

**Top drugstore: Watsons flagship/Ximen branch** because its Sunday hours usually begin before most boutiques and it has broad stock. Cosmed is the comparable backup.

Useful purchases:

- Taiwanese sunscreen, sheet masks, acne patches and lip balm
- Menthol patches/balm, mosquito repellent and itch relief
- Travel toiletries, compression socks and masks
- Avoid buying unfamiliar ingestible medicine merely as a souvenir.

Keep OTC products in original labeled packaging and carry only personal-use quantities. Check active ingredients and interactions with Mom’s medicines. Do not transfer pills into unmarked bags. Prescription drugs should travel with prescription/doctor documentation. Avoid stocking up on codeine-, stimulant- or pseudoephedrine-containing products without first confirming mainland-China import rules.

## Massage and services

**Top massage: Six Star Foot Massage, Chengdu branch**—large, mainstream, family-oriented and more suitable than an unreviewed upstairs storefront. Prebook five places; reconfirm current address, Sunday hours, exact price, card surcharge and elevator access 48 hours beforehand.

Safe alternatives:

1. **Scalp treatment/Taiwanese shampoo and blowout:** approximately **45–75 minutes, NT$600–1,500**. Best non-massage service, but reserve an English-capable salon.
2. **Basic manicure:** **45–60 minutes, NT$500–1,000**; gel removal/application takes longer and can overrun.
3. **Eyebrow shaping:** **15–30 minutes, NT$300–700**; avoid first-time tinting or microblading before flying.
4. **Korean-style photo booth:** **10–15 minutes, about NT$150–250/session**; safest spontaneous group option.
5. **Ear cleaning:** only a clearly identified professional studio using disposable/sterilized tools; skip if there is pain, infection, tubes, prior ear surgery or anticoagulant use.

## Hair removal

Waxing/sugaring is technically possible the same morning but is not recommended immediately before a flight because heat, friction and prolonged sitting can aggravate irritation. If done, choose a dedicated salon, disclose medications/retinoids, wear loose clothing, and avoid fragrance, hot baths, swimming, sun and exfoliation for 24–48 hours.

**Skip laser/IPL on departure day.** It needs consultation, sun-avoidance and aftercare and may cause redness or swelling; schedule it several days earlier or after the trip.

**Top overall picks:** Carrefour Guilin; Watsons Ximen; Six Star Chengdu; prebooked scalp shampoo/blowout.
