## 💳 Fidelity Debit Card + How Much Cash to Bring into Taiwan

### Verified conclusion (2026-08)
- **Yes, it should work in Taiwan.** The Fidelity® Debit Card is a Visa debit card. Fidelity states that eligible accounts are reimbursed when the card is used at any ATM displaying the **Visa® / Plus® / Star®** logos. In Taiwan, prioritize ATMs from **Bank of Taiwan (BOT, Taoyuan arrivals), CTBC (including many 7-Eleven ATMs), Taishin, E.SUN, Cathay United, and Chunghwa Post**. Sources: Fidelity debit card page (https://www.fidelity.com/spend-save/atm-debit-card) and Visa ATM Locator (https://www.visa.com/locator/atm).
- **International ATM surcharge reimbursement: yes for CMA, automatic.** Fidelity’s wording: “For Fidelity Cash Management Account owners ... your account will automatically be reimbursed for all ATM fees charged by other institutions while using the Fidelity® Debit Card at any ATM displaying the Visa®, Plus® or Star® logos. The reimbursement will be credited to the account the same day the ATM fee is debited.” Sources: Fidelity CMA page (https://www.fidelity.com/investing/cash-management) and debit card FAQ (https://www.fidelity.com/spend-save/faqs-atm-debit-card).
- **CMA vs brokerage condition.** CMA owners qualify. Regular Fidelity Account/brokerage debit cards qualify only if coded **Premium, Active Trader VIP, Private Client Group, Wealth Management**, or former Youth Account, etc. This is based on account/customer coding, not whether cash is held in SPAXX, FDIC sweep, or another core position.
- **Cap.** Fidelity says **all ATM fees** and does not list a monthly dollar cap; plan as unlimited/no monthly cap, but keep ATM receipts and verify the same-day credit posts.
- **FX / foreign transaction markup.** The 2024-08-19 Fidelity Debit Card Agreement says Visa converts foreign-currency transactions to USD and **“No Foreign Transaction Fees. There are no foreign transaction fees.”** Net cost ≈ **Visa exchange rate/rounding** + **0% Fidelity FTF** + **local ATM surcharge if any, reimbursed for eligible CMA**. Always reject DCC and choose **TWD / Charge in TWD**. Agreement: https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/fidelity_debit_card_agreement.pdf
- **PIN / travel notice / limits.** ATM withdrawals require a PIN. Before departure, confirm the PIN, activate the physical card, and ensure settled cash is Available to Withdraw. Agreement Section 4.1 says Core Brokerage Customers generally have ATM withdrawals limited to **$1,000 or 6 transactions per Banking Day**; Higher Net Worth Investors generally **$1,500 or 8 transactions**. Your actual limit is shown at Fidelitydebitcard.com or via the number on the back of the card. Fidelity’s current public pages do not require a travel notice; turn on transaction alerts and carry a backup card.

### USD cash to carry + TWD to get on arrival
- **Bring US$500 total cash into Taiwan** for the family (clean $100s or a $100/$50 mix), mainly as emergency backup for ATM/card issues and the later Mainland China legs. Do not bring a large USD stack just for 3–4 days in Taipei.
- **On arrival, withdraw NT$15,000 at Taoyuan using the Fidelity CMA debit card** at a BOT/CTBC/Visa/Plus ATM. This is enough for 5 people’s Taipei cash needs: night markets, small vendors, temples, some taxis, and EasyCard cash top-ups. If the ATM or card limit is lower, withdraw NT$10,000–12,000 first and top up later.
- **Cash-use logic.** Most Taipei spending can go on no-foreign-transaction-fee credit cards/contactless payments; cash is mostly for night markets, street food, traditional markets, temples, occasional taxis, and EasyCard top-up.
- **Declaration thresholds.** U.S. CBP requires reporting when a family/group carries more than **US$10,000** total (https://www.cbp.gov/travel/international-visitors/kbyg/money). Taiwan requires declaration/restrictions above **US$10,000 foreign currency** or **NT$100,000**. US$500 is far below these thresholds.

### Getting TWD: Primary + Backup
1. **Primary: Fidelity CMA debit card + Taiwan ATM.** Lowest cost and easiest: Visa exchange rate, Fidelity 0% FTF, and eligible CMA ATM surcharge reimbursement the same day. Withdraw NT$10,000–15,000 each time; reject DCC.
2. **Backup: official Taoyuan airport bank counter (Bank of Taiwan / Mega Bank) exchanging USD.** Use only if the ATM/card/PIN fails. Airport bank rates are usually better than exchanging TWD in the U.S.; budget about **NT$100 per counter transaction** by posted counter policy. Exchange only **US$200–300**.
3. **Avoid: buying TWD in the U.S. before departure.** Usually the worst rate, limited availability, and inconvenient if leftover TWD continues into Mainland China.

**Final recommendation:** Bring **US$500 backup cash** into Taiwan; after landing, use the **Fidelity CMA debit card** at a Taoyuan Visa/Plus ATM to withdraw **NT$15,000**; use bank-counter exchange only as a failure backup; always choose **TWD** and reject DCC.

## 2. ATM Withdrawal Details (Best Option)

### 2.1 Airport ATM Types and Locations
Both Taoyuan T1 and T2 arrivals levels have 24-hour ATMs: **Bank of Taiwan 台湾银行 (arrival hall · 24h · usually no local operator fee for foreign cards)**, **兆丰 Mega Bank (T2 first floor)**, and **CTBC (multiple locations)**. Local bank ATMs in Taiwan usually **do not charge an extra operator fee** for foreign cards (unlike in the U.S.).

### ⚠️ Correction A: “国泰世华 CTBC” refers to two different banks
The draft treated “国泰世华 CTBC” as one bank — **incorrect**: **国泰世华 Cathay United Bank (CUB, green logo, common in 7-Eleven)** and **CTBC Bank (red/green logo)** are two separate banks. Both have ATMs at Taoyuan Airport, and both usually do not charge an extra operator fee for foreign cards.

### 2.2 Fees and Limits
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Local ATM operator fee (BOT/CTBC/国泰世华 Cathay United) | **NT$0** |
| Foreign ATM withdrawal fee charged by your own U.S. card issuer | Depends on the card (see Section 3) |
| Per-withdrawal limit (BOT) | **NT$20,000** (daily maximum NT$100,000) |

### 2.3 ⚠️ DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion) Warning
If the ATM asks “Settle in USD? / Convert to USD?” → **always choose “Settle in TWD / Charge in TWD” and reject DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion)**. Choosing USD is usually **3–7%** worse, wasting money for no reason.

## 4. Bank Counters at Taiwan Airports (Backup Exchange Option)
- **Location**: T1 and T2 arrival halls, **24h** (both BOT and 兆丰 Mega are available).
- **Exchange rate**: Close to city-bank rates and far better than private kiosks in European/U.S. airports.
- **Fee**: about **NT$100** per transaction, subject to the posted counter fee.
- **Recommendation**: On arrival day, exchange only starter cash of **NT$5,000–8,000** (about US$155–250), and use ATMs for the rest. **Use the official Bank of Taiwan 台湾银行 window; do not use private small kiosks.** Bring your passport.

## 10. Declarations and Regulations
- **Leaving the U.S. (CBP)**: carrying **> US$10,000** in cash/equivalent monetary instruments must be declared (FinCEN 105); failure to declare may result in seizure.
- **Entering Taiwan**: foreign currency **> US$10,000** must be declared; New Taiwan dollars **> NT$100,000** must be declared (limit NT$100,000); RMB limit is RMB 20,000.
- **For this trip, 5 people carrying NT$12,000–16,000 or equivalent USD (about US$400–500) is far below all thresholds, so no declaration is needed.** ✅
